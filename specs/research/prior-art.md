### 1.2 Per-tool notes

**Portfolio Visualizer.** The class leader for allocation backtests; monthly data is its defining limit. Its Market Timing Models page is the closest thing to "conditional" investing anyone offers, but the models move the whole portfolio in and out of risk assets on one signal rather than gating purchases, and they cannot be stacked. The free tier shrank in the 2024 redesign (forum reports of a 10-year cap on recent start dates). Needs checking: exact cashflow frequency options and inflation-adjustment toggle.

**testfol.io.** Daily data and simulated long-history tickers make it the strongest free general backtester; cashflow legs are calendar-periodic. No schedule picker finer than monthly surfaced; no conditional buys.

**Curvo.** Notable for explicitly framing DCA vs lump sum as a first-class comparison and for publishing its assumptions (perfect tracking, TER, EUR, month-end). Good model for an assumptions footer.

**dcabtc.com / dca-cc.com.** The pattern to borrow is the one-screen result: total invested, current value, average cost, profit, versus lump sum, as a single large number with a line chart. Both are single-asset-class.

**Of Dollars and Data.** "Even God Couldn't Beat DCA" (2019): $100/month into the S&P 500, 40-year windows starting 1920-1980; "Buy the Dip" waits in cash and buys only at the exact bottom between two all-time highs (perfect foresight). Reported results: Buy the Dip underperforms DCA in more than 70% of 40-year windows; if you miss the bottom by two months it underperforms 97% of the time; best relative run for Buy the Dip was 1963-1983 (+29% vs DCA). The 2020/2021 follow-up uses 20-year windows 1920-2020 with dip thresholds: a 10-20% threshold beats DCA only about one time in four, a 50% threshold "nearly 40%" of the time, median shortfall about 5% at 10% threshold and about 13% at 50% threshold (the 13% figure is from a secondary PSG summary). The 70%/97% figures were visible only through a secondary copy of the article; check the primary.

**Vanguard 2012.** Shtekhman, Tasopoulos, Wimmer. Lump sum beat 12-month DCA in 67% (US), 67% (UK), 66% (AU) of rolling 10-year windows for 60/40 (one secondary source says 68% for AU). Average margin reported as 1.5%-2.4% depending on market; the "2.3%" often quoted for the US needs checking. Lengthening DCA to 36 months pushed lump-sum's win rate to roughly 90%.

**Vanguard 2023.** Lump sum beat 3-month cost averaging 68% of the time on MSCI World 1976-2022; lump sum beat cash 70%, cost averaging beat cash 69%. Paper says cost averaging can suit investors with very high loss aversion. One source attributes a 1.8% average advantage for 60/40 to this paper.

### 1.3 What nothing out there does

1. **Compose a purchase rule from a schedule plus market-state conditions.** Every tool is "amount + fixed interval." The only conditional logic anywhere (Portfolio Visualizer timing models, Faber-style MA rules) toggles the whole portfolio, is single-signal, and is monthly.
2. **Event-calendar schedules.** Nobody offers "day after FOMC," "jobs-report day," "full moon," "third Thursday," "option-expiration week," or even "every Tuesday" as a purchase schedule. Seasonax shows seasonal averages but does not turn them into a buying plan and does not compare against DCA.
3. **Per-option deltas in the add menu.** No tool previews, for each rule you could add, how the outcome would move. This is the Streak King mechanic and it has no analogue in finance tools found here.
4. **Rank many securities/sectors by how a composed rule did.** Backtesters take one portfolio at a time; none ranks the universe under a rule.
5. **Honest anomaly-decay framing.** CXO Advisory and Quantpedia re-test anomalies but as text; no tool shows "this rule looked great until 1990 and flat since" inline.

### 2.2 Condition-side evidence (the "only when" filters)

| Condition | Evidence found | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Below 200-day / 10-month moving average | Faber (2007), J. Important framing for the spec: the lump-sum literature compares *investing a windfall now vs spreading it*, while Maggiulli, Schwab and this site compare *schedules for money that arrives over time*. In the second framing "lump sum" means "invest each year's contribution on day one" (Schwab's "Ashley"), not "invest 35 years of contributions in 1990." The site should define its lump-sum benchmark explicitly and probably offer both.

## 4. Conclusions

### 4.1 Differentiation

The field splits into (a) allocation backtesters with monthly cashflows (Portfolio Visualizer, testfol.io, Curvo, PortfoliosLab), (b) single-asset "amount + interval" DCA calculators (dcabtc, dca-cc and dozens of clones), (c) seasonality chart tools (Seasonax) and strategy encyclopedias (Quantpedia, CXO), and (d) one-off articles with fixed scenarios (Maggiulli, Vanguard, Schwab, JPM). Nothing combines a user-composed schedule, market-state conditions, a ranked universe, and a per-option delta preview. The composer-plus-delta-menu is the same gap Streak King filled in sports, and it is open here. The second differentiator is honesty about decay: every anomaly above has a "then it faded" or "then someone showed it was data-snooping" chapter, and no tool shows that inline. A "before/after 1990" or rolling-window toggle on every result would be unique and is what the academic literature (Sullivan, Timmermann & White) says is the right posture.

### 4.2 UX worth borrowing

- **Curvo**: explicit assumptions footer (index as proxy, dividends reinvested, costs, data frequency). Copy this; it preempts most "your numbers are wrong" feedback.
- **dcabtc / dca-cc**: one big number (ending value), one line chart, invested-vs-value, average cost. Keep the hero simple; the composer is the complexity budget.
- **Schwab five investors**: named personas (perfect, immediate, DCA, worst day, cash) make the comparison legible. The site's fixed benchmarks could be "Plain DCA," "Lump sum," and "Stayed in cash," always shown.
- **Maggiulli**: show the *distribution* of outcomes across start dates, not one path; and show "how often does this beat plain DCA" as the headline, since win-rate is what every writeup in this literature leads with.
- **Portfolio Visualizer timing models**: the idea of listing many pre-built rules with their stats side by side is close to the "+" menu; theirs lacks composition and deltas.
- **Hartford / JPM**: the "best days cluster with worst days" chart is the single most-shared visual in this genre; a version of it that highlights which best days a given rule caught or missed would be shareable.
- **CXO Advisory**: subperiod breakdowns for every anomaly. Adopt as a standard panel.

### 4.3 What people care about, by frequency of coverage

Ranked by how often the topic surfaced across searches and how many mainstream outlets carry it (rough, from this session's results):

1. **Lump sum vs DCA** (Vanguard's two-thirds): dominant; cited by nearly every personal-finance outlet.
2. **Missing the 10 best days / timing is impossible** (JPM, Hartford, Schwab, Fidelity): the second-most-shared chart in retail finance.
3. **Buy the dip vs DCA** (Maggiulli): heavily reposted (Ritholtz, Betashares, JPM "Should I wait to buy the dip?", Coutts).
4. **Sell in May / Best Six Months**: annual press cycle every May and October.
5. **Santa Claus rally**: annual press cycle every December.
6. **Presidential cycle**: every election year.
7. **Monday effect / best day of the week**: steady trickle, mostly trading-education sites.
8. **Turn-of-month, FOMC drift, option-expiration week**: quant-blog and academic audience; FOMC drift is the one with a mainstream "it vanished" story.
9. **Full moon, Friday the 13th, daylight saving, weather**: novelty coverage, high shareability, low stakes. These are the Streak King-style hooks ("in November") that make the composer fun, and the literature conveniently says they are mostly null, which makes them safe to ship.

### 4.4 Spec implications

- Data: daily total-return series from ~1990 for the S&P 500, sectors and chosen tickers; VIX from 1990; FOMC meeting dates (Fed site publishes historical calendars); BLS employment-report release dates; CBOE/OCC option expiration calendar (third Friday rule plus holiday shifts); NYSE holiday calendar; lunar phase (computable); DST change dates (rule changed in 2007).
- Benchmarks must be defined precisely (plain DCA on the same calendar cadence; "lump sum" as invest-each-period's-contribution-immediately vs invest-everything-at-start; cash with a T-bill yield or zero).
- Cash drag is the whole story for conditional rules; the engine must track uninvested cash and probably offer "idle cash earns T-bills" as a toggle, otherwise every sparse schedule looks terrible for the wrong reason.
- Show win-rate across rolling start dates, not a single path, for any gate comparison to be meaningful.
- Carry the decay story: a before/after split or rolling window is what separates this from a seasonality chart tool.

### 4.5 Items to verify first (in priority order)

1. Vanguard 2012 PDF: exact win rates per market and the average margin (the "2.3%" is unconfirmed).
2. Maggiulli 2019: the 70% and 97% figures were seen only via a secondary copy.
3. Schwab study: which edition produced $138,044 / $127,506 / $124,248, and the exact worst-timing and cash figures.
4. Portfolio Visualizer: current free-tier limits and the exact cashflow frequency options.
5. testfol.io: data start year for simulated tickers and cashflow frequencies.
6. lazyportfolioetf.com: whether it has any DCA tool at all and what its history depth is.
7. McConnell & Xu daily figures and whether a post-2005 update exists.
8. Kamstra, Kramer & Levi 2000 effect size and Pinegar's specific objection.
9. Savor & Wilson 2013 sample period and whether the published version reports 11.4 bp.
10. Quad-witching day average return on modern data (nothing found).
